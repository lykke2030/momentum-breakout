How the setup works

Three stages, in order. A stock has to pass each one before the next is checked.

01

Prior momentum

A gap up on heavy relative volume, a thrust bar (a range of 1.5× ATR or more that closes in its top quarter), or orderly, persistent buying: 5 of 6 higher closes above rising EMAs.

02

Base and contraction

A pullback of at least 0.5 ATR that retraces less than 61.8% of the leg. Then 3 tight bars: small ranges, tight closes, EMA4 and EMA9 converging, holding above EMA21. An inside bar is noted but not required.

03

Range expansion

A bar that closes above the contraction high with at least 1.5× the contraction's average range, closing in its top 25%. The trade enters at the next bar's open, with the stop at the contraction low.

On top of that, a daily filter makes the universe a point-in-time "1-month momentum" list: a stock only qualifies on days when its prior-day 21-day return was at least 10% and it closed above its daily 21 EMA. Volume and range are compared with the same time of day on previous days.

Which part of the setup matters?

Each row switches one part off (or changes one setting) and reruns the whole test. If "breakout only" did as well as the full setup, the filters would be adding nothing.

Where does it win or lose?

Full-setup trades split by one feature at a time (fixed exit). Groups under about 30 trades are noise. Sorting through many groups will always turn up something that looks good by chance.

Costs and stop width

The stop sits at the contraction low, which is often very close to the entry. With 5 bps of slippage on each side, a tight stop means costs eat a large share of each R.

Method

No lookahead

  • A signal is confirmed when its bar closes. The trade enters at the next bar's open.
  • Relative volume and typical range use earlier days only.
  • The daily filter uses the previous day's close.
  • Check 1: on random-walk prices, every variant lost about the cost of trading.
  • Check 2: a textbook setup planted in the data was detected on the correct bar.

Execution

  • 5 bps slippage per side. Stop-first if a bar touches both the stop and the target.
  • One trade at a time per symbol. Every trade is closed by the end of the session.
  • Out-of-sample = the most recent 30% of trading days.

Limits

  • The 116-stock list was picked in late 2026, so it leans toward names that already did well (hindsight bias). The daily filter limits this but doesn't remove it.
  • About 13 months of 5-minute history. The mega-cap set covers 5.5 years but rarely passes the momentum filter.
  • The settings are reasonable guesses, not tuned. The original indicator's thresholds aren't public.
  • This is research, not trading advice.

Run it yourself

The backtester is one Python file. It downloads bars from Alpaca (historical SIP data is free when it's more than 15 minutes old), caches them, and writes the same report.
